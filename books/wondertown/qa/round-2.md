Frozen blind report: 2026-10-24. No game source, design, maps, earlier reports, walkthroughs, tests, or save contents were read before this report and transcript were saved. Play used only currently offered numeric links, with complete VM persistence at `/tmp/wondertown-r2.sav`. ## Result

No ending reached. Explored Workshop Floor, Tool Bench, Snowy Alley, Clock Square, Mailbox Corner, and Scrap-Yard. Three distinct progression routes became blocked by missing actions in the player-visible menus. The transcript is the authoritative command and output record. No typed-command or state bypass was attempted.

## High: necessary repair actions absent

- Workshop: examined the lifting mechanism and ladder. Both explicitly need oil. Found the oil can by looking under the workbench, took it, examined it, and checked inventory. There is no oil, pour, or use choice. The offered `turn mechanism with can` says: “Pip strains against the mechanism, but it is rusted solid. The iron needs oil first.” The clue is fair; the menu cannot perform the indicated action.
- Tool Bench: examined and spoke to the frozen nutcracker. TALK says: “His painted mouth stays clamped shut. He looks like he has plenty to say, if only someone would wind him up.” Examined and took his winding key. No wind/turn choice appears for nutcracker or key, including after inventory. The route upward is explicitly blocked by the frozen captain.
- Scrap-Yard: examining the piles reveals a porcelain head; examining the cart reveals a headless doll. Descriptions clearly explain that they match. Took both and inspected them, but no attachment or repair action appears. The cart blocks the iron gate and cannot be pushed. The player can identify a promising compassionate solution but cannot try it.

## Medium: menus invite irrelevant or invalid commands

Binary choices grow into a long list of combinations with scenery and unrelated objects: opening the pet door with sawdust, turning the mechanism with the clock, attacking the cart with the gate. Necessary repair verbs are missing while dozens of implausible combinations compete for attention. `open cart` is explicitly offered yet prints “You must tell me how to do that to a scrap-metal cart.” Current menus also have awkward visible labels such as “Go north north” and “Turn with oil can lifting mechanism”.

## First-experience writing and fairness

The snow, cedar, tiny lantern, repaired cart, and unsent toy letters establish a coherent, gentle world with stakes. Hidden objects are revealed through clear physical investigation, and the toolbox's small scale feels convincing. Tolliver's letter gives emotional purpose; the toy-rescue discovery encourages kindness without lecturing. Mailbox conversation about the footprints is useful and charming.

The opening launched directly into a room without explaining who Pip is or the immediate objective; the empty hook and eastward footprints suggested a theft, while the letter much later explained the heart. An introductory sentence would help a new reader understand why they act. “The key looks like it would fit perfectly” lacks a stated destination. The upward crate/chair/books route is well described but has no offered climb before the captain wakes; presenting a blocked climb would better connect the puzzle to the spatial goal.

This remains a partial-world literary assessment: unseen passages and ending are unassessed.
