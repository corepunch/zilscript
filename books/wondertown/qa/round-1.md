## Craft and clarity

The physical scale, cedar shavings, repaired toys and Grandfather's pop-up book give the opening a coherent, warm identity. The repair-book clue “follow the ticking beneath the ticking” is memorable. The envelope gives a credible personal goal and a missing-grandfather mystery. However the initial frame never says who Pip is or what triggered the emergency; the player begins amid unexplained urgency. The clock says time is running out but no concrete time is visible; slow examination risks feeling punished. Some objects are described in second person (“your tiny frame”), others refer to Pip in third person. This should settle on one viewpoint. The horse's broken leg is evocative; the doll counterpart needed for the first puzzle is not described until the detached head mentions it, weakening fair discovery. The mailbox footprint description suggests south, but that room offers west only: specify that the tracks double back west to the square before turning south.
