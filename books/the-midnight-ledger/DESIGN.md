# The Midnight Ledger

The berth auction appears to end in an ordinary accusation against a porter. Instead the player separates three facts: what changed, when it changed, and who could reach it. Contemporary physical evidence, not confession or character, comes first. Ives begins defensive, disputes the impossible clock, then asks to be judged by the latch. Orr enjoys a signature that does not cover the right time. Venn signals the standard of proof rather than naming the answer.

Map: Vestibule ↔ Cloakroom, Vestibule ↔ Hearing Chamber. The hearing joins Weigh Room, Records Room and Public Registry. Records leads to the Transit Cage. There are no irreversible movements, timers or deaths.

The main evidence can be read in any order. Turning the panel early identifies the missing category without declaring guilt. Both named choices appear only after the reconstruction. An unsupported finding against Ives neither consumes evidence nor ends the story; it identifies its contradiction. The ending demonstrates the missing object physically returned to the room.

Optional investigations of the stopped clock, the transit crate and the witnesses add texture and a second lead but do not substitute for primary evidence. Link tests check early rejection, all seven rooms and a recoverable false accusation before the supported finding.
