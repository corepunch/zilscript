# The Station at Minus One

The last person in a transit station beneath a frozen moon has to decide whether to answer a train scheduled to arrive yesterday. The station's detector measures whether history will accept a new stop; it does not prove who is aboard. A formal rescue opens a disputed new timeline, while a quarantine can still warn the other driver through the one medium that crosses the fault: sound in the rail. The player gets to make a careful decision under pressure, not guess a magic word.

Engineering suspense for teens and adults, around 12 turns on a straight route. No lethal clock or unwinnable item state. Both endings require the player to tell an empty reflection from a train by conducting a repeatable experiment.

| Place | Exits | Apparatus / purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticket Hall | west to Staff Room; east to Gauge Room; north or down to Platform | Station clock: shows the anomaly |
| Staff Room | east to Hall | Logbook explains tomorrow's empty test; ceramic puck is portable |
| Gauge Room | west to Hall | Receiver cradle, calibration stud: test the insulated puck, compare the live mass |
| Lower Platform | south or up to Hall | Rescue switch and quarantine latch; two in-world tradeoffs |

Calibration requires the puck in the cradle; taking it out doesn't erase already observed comparison. Using a control without the test gives a specific reason, not a hidden door. A second test is safe, and both choices remain visible after proof. The parser accepts compass directions and names of the visible apparatus.
