# The Pocket Weather Bureau
Stage 1. Original co-play adventure, age 8+, 2026.

You are the newest weather-post apprentice in a town small enough to measure rain in a thimble. Your first forecast has escaped. The cloud is hiding, and the official instruction is to bring it back.

Core fantasy: learn to observe a living weather system well enough to make a promise both the town and its cloud can keep.

12 connected rooms, roughly 45-65 purposeful actions; five integrated puzzles and a public-resolution sequence, four characters, no death, countdown, expendable key or irreversible wrong turn. Play is physical: search, trade, turn, dig, steam paper, tie a ribbon, listen, show evidence, revise and deliver a forecast. The world is a simulation, not a sequence of answer buttons.

Opening: brass dispatch bell; apprentice's forecast card; a silent vane and runaway cloud; immediate bell response. Mica treats the rain as a delivery problem. The midpoint report reveals that the cloud once obeyed an instruction even when a frightened child asked it to stop. The final task changes from obedience to consent. A pause rule matters as much as the route.

Win: restore the cloud's confidence, route rain into the garden, publish an approved forecast, then choose a soft drizzle or a puddle-making shower. Both endings honor the pause rule. No lose state. Optional: recover the old report's pressed flower, hear the rook's weather joke, and learn the baker's emergency biscuit policy.

Fairness: all special verbs exposed on actual objects, concrete nouns linked, short sentences, ASCII parser vocabulary, progressive HINT control. Standard syntax reused. Every tool is unique; wrong explicit tools never borrow a correct carried tool. No extra scene engine or timers: characters react/move on meaningful events.
