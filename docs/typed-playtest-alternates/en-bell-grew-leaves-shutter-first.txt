The Bell That Grew Leaves
Studio Null, 2026
Release 1

Three soft taps come up through the fog: tap, tap, tap. That is the village across the hollow, asking whether your village is still there. Your old bell is made of wood, too brittle to strike, and for the first time the grown-ups have left it to you to answer. You have come up to the Bell Court to find a way.

Bell Court
You are in the Bell Court, a small cobbled yard at the top of the village. Fog beads on the low wall. One cobble rocks under your shoe with a tiny clack. In the middle hangs the old root bell, carved from wood and low enough to touch, with pale roots growing down around its rim. The reed store is through a low doorway to the east, and the spring walk lies uphill to the north.

> east
This is the Reed Store, a narrow wooden shed that smells like a field in August. Tied bundles of reeds lean in the corners, and a painted signal tile hangs on the wall above a plain shelf. The only way out is back west to the bell court.
A short reed wick has been laid on the shelf underneath the tile.

> take wick
Taken.

> north
You are on the Spring Walk, a mossy ledge where a cold spring bubbles out of the hillside. A brass turning spout pokes out of the rock beside a shallow stone soaking bowl. The bowl is dry, though you can hear water running away somewhere nearby. The bell court is back down the hill to the south, and stone steps lead east to the shutter landing.

> turn spout
You turn the spout with a squeak. Water gushes into the soaking bowl, and the bowl fills faster than the crack leaks.

> put wick in bowl
You dip the reed wick in the water. It goes dark from one end to the other as it drinks. You lift it out before it can float away.

> east
This is the Shutter Landing, a little stone platform at the edge of the village. Here the wall ends and the white sky seems to start below your feet. A closed frost shutter, square and wooden, covers the wall that faces the hollow. Frost glitters at its corners, and everything is very quiet. The stone steps lead back west to the spring walk.

> open shutter
You lift the peg and swing the shutter open. Fog comes in as quietly as a cat.

> west
You are on the Spring Walk, a mossy ledge where a cold spring bubbles out of the hillside. A brass turning spout pokes out of the rock beside a shallow stone soaking bowl. Water pours into the bowl, and the bowl holds a shaking picture of the sky. The bell court is back down the hill to the south, and stone steps lead east to the shutter landing.

> south
You are in the Bell Court, a small cobbled yard at the top of the village. Fog beads on the low wall. One cobble rocks under your shoe with a tiny clack. In the middle hangs the old root bell, carved from wood and low enough to touch, with pale roots growing down around its rim. A cool draft drifts down from the north and stirs the roots. The reed store is through a low doorway to the east, and the spring walk lies uphill to the north.

> put wick in bell
You push the reed wick into the hole, and the root takes hold of it. There's enough left showing to pull it free again.

> ring bell
The bell makes no sound at all. Water climbs the wet wick and three small leaves uncurl from the rim. All three let go and sail through the shutter. The first turns its white side up, then the second, then the third. You wait until your fingers go cold. Then, in the far fog, three white leaves turn over in answer. Someone has been standing out in the cold with you this whole time.

THE END -- THREE LEAVES FOR YES
The bell has a clear path across the fog.
