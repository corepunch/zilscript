The Bell That Grew Leaves
Studio Null, 2026
Release 1

Three soft taps come up through the fog: tap, tap, tap. That is the village across the hollow, asking whether your village is still there. Your old bell is made of wood, too brittle to strike, and for the first time the grown-ups have left it to you to answer. You have come up to the Bell Court to find a way.

Bell Court
You are in the Bell Court, a small cobbled yard at the top of the village. Fog beads on the low wall. One cobble rocks under your shoe with a tiny clack. In the middle hangs the old root bell, carved from wood and low enough to touch, with pale roots growing down around its rim. The reed store is through a low doorway to the east, and the spring walk lies uphill to the north.

> examine bell
The bell is carved from one piece of old wood, not metal, so hitting it hard would only crack it. Pale roots grow down around its rim, dry and thin. One of them has a small round hole in it, just the right size for a wick.

> examine cobble
Round grey cobbles, green with moss between them, and a low stone wall all around. The yard is just big enough for the whole village to stand in when the bell is rung, though today it's only you.

> east
This is the Reed Store, a narrow wooden shed that smells like a field in August. Tied bundles of reeds lean in the corners, and a painted signal tile hangs on the wall above a plain shelf. The only way out is back west to the bell court.
A short reed wick has been laid on the shelf underneath the tile.

> read tile
There are no words on the tile, only three painted pictures in a row, like a lesson for someone too young to read. First, a reed wick standing in a bowl of water. Second, the wick tucked into a hole in the bell's roots. Third, three white leaves floating out through an open shutter. Underneath, someone painted a fourth leaf, then thought better of it and crossed it out.

> ring bell
Water climbs up the wet wick into the wood, and with a soft rustle a tiny pale leaf unfolds on the rim. It leans away toward the hollow, then stops and droops. Something square is holding the fog in, and the leaf has nowhere to go.

In the middle hangs the old root bell, carved from wood and low enough to touch, with pale roots growing down around its rim. A reed wick hangs from a hole in one of the roots. One pale leaf still clings to the rim. A cool draft drifts down from the north and stirs the roots. The reed store is through a low doorway to the east, and the spring walk lies uphill to the north.

> ring bell
The bell makes no sound at all. The drooping leaf lifts into the draft. Two more uncurl beside it. All three let go and sail through the shutter. The first turns its white side up, then the second, then the third. You wait until your fingers go cold. Then, in the far fog, three white leaves turn over in answer. Someone has been standing out in the cold with you this whole time.

THE END -- THREE LEAVES FOR YES
The bell has a clear path across the fog.
