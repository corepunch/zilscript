The Museum of Unsent Signals
AdventureArena, 2026
Release 1

The museum is closing for good. Tonight is the last night of the Museum of Unsent Signals, the place where the city kept every message that was recorded and never sent. Tomorrow morning the old relay under the dome will be switched off. The caretaker left you in charge of the last shift. There is a note on the plaque and one piece of advice: “You don't have to make everything speak.”

Admissions
You're in the admissions lobby. Only one light is still on, above the ticket counter and a brass plaque. Beneath the hum of the relay, you can hear each of your own steps on the tiles. The Dead Letters gallery lies east, the Quiet Hall west, and the signal booth north.

> east
You're in the Dead Letters gallery. The glass cases run down both walls. At first the dark glass shows you only your own reflection; the inspection lens on its stand was put there for a closer look. The door back to admissions is west.
One wooden spool has been taken out of its case and left beside the lens, with a paper tag tied to it.

> take spool
Taken.

> tilt lens
You tilt the lens toward the ceiling light. Fine scratches on the glass, invisible a moment ago, catch the light and shine: an old instruction from the relay staff. Three cuts on the wooden edge mean RETURN ONLY. No public forwarding. Someone has underlined the word edge.

> west
You're in the admissions lobby. Only one light is still on, above the ticket counter and a brass plaque. Beneath the hum of the relay, you can hear each of your own steps on the tiles. The Dead Letters gallery lies east, the Quiet Hall west, and the signal booth north.

> north
You are in the signal booth, a small glass room at the heart of the relay. The relay hums under your feet. The glass is warm enough to lean your forehead against. Set into the desk is a shallow reading basin for playing spools, with a round audition stud beside it. Below them are two sending keys: the return key on the left and the memorial key on the right, with a little address indicator above them. The corridor leads back south to admissions.

> put spool in basin
You set the spool into the basin. It catches and starts to turn, and the audition stud begins to glow on the basin's rim.

> push stud
A woman's voice says, “I made it.” Then, for a whole minute, you hear nothing but her breathing, slow and steady, as if she is waiting for someone to answer. The address indicator matches the three cuts on the spool to the private return register: this was never a public announcement. She was talking to one person, whoever used to wait up for her. Below the basin, both sending keys light up. One offers an uncertain private reply; the other shares only silence with the room. Neither will send her voice.

> push memorial key
You press the key and send the minute without the voice. Across the dome, every speaker announces nothing at all. You had expected complaints asking what went wrong. Instead, the night dispatcher sends one short message: “Please wait to reset. Someone is still listening.”

ENDING: A MINUTE FOR THE LIVING
The museum has entered its last exhibit.
