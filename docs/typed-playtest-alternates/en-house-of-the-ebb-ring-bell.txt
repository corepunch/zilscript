House of the Ebb
Studio Null, 2026
Release 1

You have come home to the tide-house, the old ferry-keeper's house out on the rocks, for the first time in nine winters. Your younger brother Kit promised he would wait for you on the causeway, and tonight he was there, just as he said, his boots ringing on the stones exactly like yours. No one has kept the house since the last ferry was lost. Yet a light is burning in its window.

Drowned Causeway
You are on the causeway, a raised stone road across the tidal flats that the sea covers twice a day. Seaweed has dried into the mortar between the stones in little commas, and the water sucks at them on either side. Kit walks a pace behind you, close enough to touch. Ahead to the north stands the tide-house where you grew up, and someone has left a light burning in a house that has never had electricity.

> north
Kit sets his bag beside the bench.
This is the waiting hall, where ferry passengers once sat out the weather until the boat came. Rain has blown in through a broken pane and settles in the crease of a long wooden bench. Your brother Kit stands by the bench, watching rainwater creep toward his bag. Stairs lead north and up into the house, and from them comes the sound of a boot being set down very carefully. The cold kitchen is to the west, and the tally office, which smells of vinegar, is to the east. The causeway is back to the south.

> east
This is the tally office, where the keeper counted every passenger out on the ferry and every passenger back. It smells sharply of vinegar, and the damp has got into everything. An old tally sheet is pinned to a board on the wall, its corners curled with damp. The only way out is back west to the hall.

> read sheet
The sheet records the ferry's last crossing. Three names went out on the boat, and only two were counted back. The keeper did not count the third; he struck the line out instead. In the margin, in your own handwriting, are the words “I said the third was never there.” Under the struck line, the initials K. R. can still be made out.

> west
In the kitchen, a strip of moonlight crosses the black iron range where there used to be a fire. One small white child's mug sits on the ledge, clean around the lip; the rest of the shelf is furred with dust. The hall, a little warmer, is back to the east.

> examine mug
The white glaze is chipped where Kit always knocked his teeth on it. Someone has scraped his initials off the side. But under the handle, where no one else would think to look, he scratched them in again himself: K. R.

> north
You are on the landing at the top of the stairs. Drops of water are creeping UP the wall here, one bead at a time, against all sense. A brass signal plate of instructions is screwed to the wall. The stairs go back down to the hall to the south, and a short ladder climbs up to the lantern room.

> read plate
Instructions for a search at sea, stamped in brass. OPEN the seaward shutter. TURN the lens until it holds the missing berth, the place where the lost boat should be, in its light. Then, on the gallery, RING the bell to call the shore as witnesses, or PULL the brake to keep a private vigil instead. NEVER sound the bell into an unlit sea.

> up
This is the lantern room at the top of the house, a small glass room built to signal the ferry. In the middle stands the great signal lens, and it turns the rain on the windows violet. A narrow shutter in the seaward wall is shut tight against the sea. You can climb back down to the landing, and the outside gallery runs off to the east.

> open shutter
You lift the catch and swing the shutter out. The wind steps into the room like somebody late for dinner. It smells of Kit's wet coat.

> turn lens
You turn the glass to the extra notch at the end of the rim. The beam swings out over the water and settles on an empty berth, where no boat is. On the stair below you hear the boot buckle you heard on the causeway, and you realize Kit is no longer with you. Out on the gallery to the east, someone is calling his name in your voice.

> east
You are on the outside gallery, a narrow walkway that runs around the top of the house under a little roof. The wind up here sounds almost homely, like someone humming to themselves in the next room. A brass witness bell hangs just within reach. Beside it stands Kit, his thumb resting on the bell's clapper brake. The lantern room is back to the west.

> ring bell
The first note is ugly. The second wakes a light across the water. Between the second note and the third you shout, “Kit Rook was aboard! I let you write him out.” You stay on the gallery until the first lantern ashore turns this way. Someone on shore will have to open the tally and find the space where his name was scraped away.
