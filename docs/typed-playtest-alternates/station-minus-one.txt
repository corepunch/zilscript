The Station at Minus One
AdventureArena, 2026
Release 1

The last lift to the surface left an hour ago. You are alone on the night shift at the colony's deepest railway station, far under the ice of a frozen moon. You have locked the gates open and rinsed your cup. Then the platform bell rings. A train is asking to stop, and by the timetable it was due yesterday. It is calling in from the wrong side of midnight, and somebody has to decide whether to let it in.

Ticket Hall
You stand in the ticket hall, a box of concrete under the ice. The ceiling lamps are off; green glow strips light your breath from below. Above the ticket wickets, the station clock keeps creeping toward midnight. The staff room is to the west and the track instruments are in the gauge room to the east. Steps lead north to the lower platform.

> east
This is the gauge room, where the station weighs whatever stands on its track. It is bitterly cold, and the floor hums at the edge of hearing. Frost furs the old dials and pipes. Only two things on the console are clear of it: a round receiver cradle, and a square calibration stud beside it. The ticket hall is back to the west. The cradle and stud offer a way to weigh the caller before you commit the platform controls.

EMPTY REFERENCE, says the little window over the stud. The gauge has nothing to measure against; you need to seat a ceramic puck in the cradle first.

> examine cradle
A round hollow in the console with one notch in its rim, sized for a standard ceramic weight. The stud beside it can't run a test until the cradle holds one.

> put puck in cradle
The puck settles into the cradle with a ceramic click. Beside it, the calibration stud glows once: the gauge has its reference weight.

> press stud
The needle sweeps twice. First reading: nothing. Second reading: the full weight of a train, on a track where you can see no train. Then the last digits flicker by a few kilos, the way a scale does when a person shifts from one foot to the other. A reflection cannot do that. Someone is aboard.

> north

Two controls stand on a post at the platform edge: a red rescue switch and a grey quarantine latch. The steps back up to the ticket hall are to the south.

> pull quarantine latch
The latch only presses in. Once pressed, it would lock.

> press latch
You press the latch, and the berth closes. A single note travels out along the rail under the frost; after a long pause, another comes back. The driver has heard you and is braking, safe in yesterday. The warmth fades from the rails. You keep your hand on the latch until the answering note has died, so the last thing you give the driver is not silence. Later, back in the staff room, you draw two small circles in the logbook for the two signals, so whoever works here alone tomorrow will know there was an answer.
