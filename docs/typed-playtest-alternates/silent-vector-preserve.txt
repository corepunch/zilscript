The Silent Vector
Studio Null, 2026
Release 1

“Keep holding her hand.” You remember saying it, and you remember a small, cold hand in yours.

You are the engineer of Asterion, a research station far out beyond the outer relay. The crew list says four people live here, and you can name them all; yet you remember a fifth, as clearly as you remember your own voice comforting her. Now something has tripped the habitat systems. You have woken alone in your sleeping cradle, with the strap loose across your chest. Your fingers are still cold.

Restraint Cradle
You are in your restraint cradle, a padded sleeping niche at the south end of the habitat. A dry click repeats somewhere inside the padding, steady as a pulse. The habitat breaker is set into the frame, within arm's reach. The pressure-balanced door to the north, which leads to the habitat junction, is sealed.

> push breaker
The breaker catches. Air moves across your face. The mechanical telltale settles at one atmosphere, and the interlock releases north. A small fact, independent of anything you remember. The door is open, but the click that woke you is gone: whatever tripped the breaker may have left other traces before you arrived.

> compare strip with comparator
Four breath loads before reception. Four after. No rupture, no fifth body. The supposed decompression has no matching pressure drop. A reception-time handstamp divides the continuous physical trace; there is no room in it for the accident you all remember.

The pressure trace stays level through the minute when you remember the accident. Something has taught you to remember it. You remember letting go of her hand, and the guilt does not lessen when the mechanism becomes clear.

> open receiver

> examine wafer
A memory wafer on a passive substrate. Its engraved index reads LENA / RESCUE-COMPRESSION / SENN ORLOV. An outer-relay return stamp crosses Asterion's own outbound serial. There is no independent power source.

You remember Lena reciting that serial while she worked. You do not remember teaching it to her. Senn can account for the name; cold storage will hold the sample without playback.

> preserve console
You commit to preservation.

The archive closes without reading the wafer. Four measured bodies; one manufactured biography. You record both facts, and the fact that neither settled the question of suffering. The sealed capsule carries its warning as physical grooves, not an enticing voice.

At the rendezvous, Mara counts four pulses. Ivo asks whether anyone remembers what Lena liked to eat. All four of you answer differently. Senn begins to apologize. You ask him to help inventory the capsule instead.

The rescue ship will not accept the capsule until its medical officer has assessed it. She proposes a test playback. You refuse. For two days you trade sleep for custody while people with clean voices argue that understanding is worth a controlled exposure. Senn gives his first statement beside the unaltered foil. Ivo takes the next watch.

Eventually they accept sealed transfer under your warning. Someone else will hold the keys tomorrow. You have saved the possibility of an answer, and taken responsibility for keeping it unanswered until an answer can be sought safely.
